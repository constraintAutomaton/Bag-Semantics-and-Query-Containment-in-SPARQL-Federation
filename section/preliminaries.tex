\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:preliminaries}

\subsection{SPARQL Language}

SPARQL~\cite{w3SPARQLQuery} is the W3C standard language for querying \ac{RDF}~\cite{w3ConceptsAbstract} \acp{KG}.
An \ac{RDF} \ac{KG} is a set of \emph{triples} $(s, p, o)$ with $s \in \mathcal{I} \cup \mathcal{B}$, $p \in \mathcal{I}$, and $o \in \mathcal{I} \cup \mathcal{B} \cup \mathcal{L}$, where $\mathcal{I}$, $\mathcal{B}$, and $\mathcal{L}$ are the sets of \acp{IRI}, blank nodes, and literals.
A \emph{triple pattern} extends a triple by allowing variables (drawn from a set $\mathcal{V}$, disjoint from $\mathcal{I}$, $\mathcal{B}$, and $\mathcal{L}$) in any position:
$\mathit{tp} = (s, p, o)$ where $s \in \mathcal{I} \cup \mathcal{B} \cup \mathcal{V}$, $p \in \mathcal{I} \cup \mathcal{V}$, and $o \in \mathcal{I} \cup \mathcal{B} \cup \mathcal{L} \cup \mathcal{V}$.

A \ac{BGP}~\cite{w3SPARQLQueryFormal} is a set of triple patterns evaluated conjunctively, binding variables across all the patterns it contains.
A \ac{GP}~\cite{w3SPARQLQueryFormal} generalizes a \ac{BGP} with additional operators; here the only such operator is union, which combines \acp{GP} into a \ac{UGP}~\cite{w3SPARQLQueryFormal}, so every \ac{BGP} and \ac{UGP} is a \ac{GP}.
A \ac{CQ} is a query whose body is a single \ac{BGP}, optionally followed by a projection.
A query's \emph{distinguished} variables are those retained by its head, that is those bound by the solution mappings it returns, and the query is \emph{projection-free} when every variable of its body is distinguished.
A \ac{UCQ} is a query whose body is a \ac{UGP} whose disjuncts are \acp{BGP}.
A \ac{CQ} is then the special case of a \ac{UCQ} with a single disjunct, so the \acp{CQ} form a subclass of the \acp{UCQ}.

Matching a \ac{GP} against an \ac{RDF} graph produces a \emph{bag} of \emph{solution mappings}, each a partial function from variables to \ac{RDF} terms.
We write $\operatorname{multiplicity}(\mu \mid \Omega)$ for the number of times a solution mapping $\mu$ occurs in a bag $\Omega$.
We define the following notions used throughout the paper.

\begin{definition}[Merge~\cite{w3SPARQLQuery}]\label{def_merge}
  Two solution mappings $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ are \emph{compatible} if $\mu_1(v)$ and $\mu_2(v)$ are the same \ac{RDF} term~\cite{w3ConceptsAbstract} for every variable $v \in \operatorname{dom}(\mu_1) \cap \operatorname{dom}(\mu_2)$.
  The \emph{merge} of two compatible solution mappings is their union, written $\operatorname{merge}(\mu_1, \mu_2) = \mu_1 \cup \mu_2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[SPARQL Union multiplicity~\cite{w3SPARQLQuery}]\label{def_union_multiplicity}
  The multiplicity of a solution mapping $\mu$ in the SPARQL \texttt{UNION} of two bags $\Omega_1$ and $\Omega_2$ is
  \begin{multline*}
  \operatorname{multiplicity}(\mu \mid \texttt{UNION}(\Omega_1, \Omega_2)) = \\
  \operatorname{multiplicity}(\mu \mid \Omega_1) + \operatorname{multiplicity}(\mu \mid \Omega_2).
  \end{multline*}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[SPARQL Join multiplicity~\cite{w3SPARQLQuery}]\label{def_join_multiplicity}
  The multiplicity of a solution mapping $\mu$ in the SPARQL \texttt{JOIN} of two bags $\Omega_1$ and $\Omega_2$ is
  \begin{multline*}
  \operatorname{multiplicity}(\mu \mid \texttt{JOIN}(\Omega_1, \Omega_2)) = \\
  \sum_{(\mu_1, \mu_2) \in m(\mu)} \operatorname{multiplicity}(\mu_1 \mid \Omega_1) \cdot \operatorname{multiplicity}(\mu_2 \mid \Omega_2),
  \end{multline*}
  where $m(\mu) = \{(\mu_1, \mu_2) \mid \mu_1 \in \Omega_1,\ \mu_2 \in \Omega_2,\ \operatorname{merge}(\mu_1, \mu_2) = \mu\}$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Federated Queries}

A SPARQL federated query involves executing different segments of a query across multiple federation members.
We denote the evaluation of a query $Q$ over a \ac{KG} $G$ as $\eval{Q}{G}$.
A set of federation members is called a \emph{federation}.
Evaluating a query over a federation $F$ involves decomposing $Q$ into subqueries $P_i$, where each subquery $P_i$ is evaluated at a federation member in $F$ containing a \ac{KG} $G_i$.
The \ac{KG} queried locally is itself a member of $F$, written $f_{\mathit{loc}}$, so that every subquery is evaluated at a member.
We adopt a database-agnostic view of federation members: since query containment does not depend on the content of any particular \ac{KG}, we formalize the notion of federation in terms of endpoint URLs.
A \texttt{SERVICE} clause, written \texttt{SERVICE :anIRI \{?s ?p ?o\}} in SPARQL syntax and denoted $SERVICE(I, P)$~\cite{w3SPARQLFederated}, where $I$ is the URL locating the federation member and $P$ is the graph pattern to evaluate, performs $P$ at the endpoint identified by $I$.
\footnote{We do not consider the \texttt{SILENT} parameter in this formalization.}

To reason about federated query plans, we use the FedQPL formalism~\cite{Cheng2021}, a more formal and extensible algebra, with the operators defined below.
FedQPL is defined under set semantics, where $\fmu$ denotes set union~\cite[Def.~6]{Cheng2021}; we reinterpret $\fmu$ and $\fmj$ as their bag counterparts, and justify that reinterpretation in \Cref{sec_fedqpl}.

\begin{definition}[FedQPL operators, adapted from~\cite{Cheng2021}]\label{def_fedqpl}
  The \emph{request} $\freq_{\mathit{fm}}^{P}$ evaluates a graph pattern $P$ against a single federation member $\mathit{fm}$.
  The \emph{multiway union} $\fmu$ and \emph{multiway join} $\fmj$ combine the results of their arguments by bag union and by join, respectively.
\end{definition}

A SPARQL $SERVICE(I, P)$ is thus expressed in FedQPL by the request $\freq_{I}^{P}$.
\footnote{We slightly simplify the formalism: FedQPL has a sophisticated representation of federation members, but for query containment a member can be represented by its URL.}
