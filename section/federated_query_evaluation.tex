\section{Conditions for Federated Query Containment}\label{sec:federated_queries_query_containment}

Federated query containment may initially appear fundamentally different from single-source query containment, since containment is data-source independent while a federated query names particular databases.
However, this difference is not fundamental.
We propose that federated query containment must consider the URLs of the federation members, but not their content.
Referencing these URLs could benefit the resolution of query containment, for instance by guaranteeing the absence of overlap between members, under which the \ac{BFR} yields sets rather than bags (\Cref{cor_no_overlap}).
We leave such optimizations out of scope.

Without loss of generality, we work only with flattened \texttt{SERVICE} clauses, where every \texttt{SERVICE} clause appears at the \ac{BGP} level.
The proposition below shows that a nested \texttt{SERVICE} clause is semantically equivalent to such a flattened form, so nesting reduces to this case.

Deciding containment for a federated query requires a form in which a query and the federation it addresses can be examined as a whole.
We obtain such a form by centralizing the federation into a single graph whose triples carry the member holding them, and by extending each triple pattern with a fourth component---its \emph{location}---naming the federation member that evaluates it.\footnote{We extend triples for readability. \ac{RDF} datasets are commonly represented as quads whose fourth component names a graph~\cite{w3ConceptsAbstract}; there the location is a fifth component and the development below is unchanged.}

\begin{definition}[Located federated database]\label{def_located_db}
  Let $F$ be a federation and $G_f$ the \ac{KG} held by member $f \in F$.
  The \emph{located database} of $F$ is
  \[
    G^{*} = \bigcup_{f \in F} \{(s,p,o,f) \mid (s,p,o) \in G_f\}.
  \]
\end{definition}

A triple held by member $f$ is thus a located triple whose location is $f$, and a triple pattern evaluated at $f$ a located triple pattern whose location is $f$; the query is rewritten accordingly.

\begin{definition}[Located form of a query]\label{def_located_form}
  Let $Q$ be a flattened federated \ac{CQ} over a federation $F$.
  The \emph{located form} $L(Q)$ of $Q$ is the \ac{CQ} with the same head as $Q$ whose body contains, for every triple pattern $(s,p,o)$ of $Q$, the located triple pattern $(s,p,o,f)$, where $f$ is the member evaluating it, namely the member of the \texttt{SERVICE} clause enclosing the pattern, or $f_{\mathit{loc}}$ if the pattern belongs to the local \ac{BGP}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop_federated_containment}
Let $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ be flattened federated \acp{CQ} over the federations $F_1$ and $F_2$, and let $L(Q)$ denote the located form of a query $Q$.
Then
\[
Q_1 \sqsubseteq Q_2 \iff L(Q_1) \sqsubseteq L(Q_2),
\]
where $\sqsubseteq$ on result bags denotes bag containment, and the right-hand side is containment of \acp{CQ} over located databases.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every federation over $F_1 \cup F_2$ has a unique located database (\Cref{def_located_db}), just as every query has a unique located form (\Cref{def_located_form}), and every located database is that of exactly one federation, obtained by grouping its located triples by their location.

Moreover $\eval{Q}{D} = \eval{L(Q)}{D^{*}}$ for every flattened federated \ac{CQ} $Q$ and every federation $D$ with located database $D^{*}$.
By \Cref{def_located_form}, a subgoal of $L(Q)$ whose location is $f$ matches in $D^{*}$ exactly the located triples whose location is $f$, hence exactly the triples that the corresponding triple pattern of $Q$ matches in the \ac{KG} $G_f$ of that member.
Locations affect neither the merge of solution mappings (\Cref{def_merge}) nor the multiplicity of a join (\Cref{def_join_multiplicity}), and $Q$ and $L(Q)$ have the same head, so both evaluations produce the same solution mappings with the same multiplicities.

Both directions of the containment equivalence then follow directly from these two facts.

($\Rightarrow$) Let $D^{*}$ be a located database, and $D$ the federation whose located database it is.
Then $\eval{L(Q_1)}{D^{*}} = \eval{Q_1}{D}$, which is a subbag of $\eval{Q_2}{D} = \eval{L(Q_2)}{D^{*}}$.

($\Leftarrow$) Let $D$ be a federation with located database $D^{*}$.
Then $\eval{Q_1}{D} = \eval{L(Q_1)}{D^{*}}$, which is a subbag of $\eval{L(Q_2)}{D^{*}} = \eval{Q_2}{D}$.
\end{proof}

A location is always an \ac{IRI}, never a variable, since we do not consider variables as endpoint URLs (\Cref{sec:related_works_fed}).
Containment thus depends on the URLs of the federation members, but not on their content, as announced above.
