\section{Semantics of the Query Results of SPARQL Queries}\label{sec:semantic_federated_q}

Before investigating query containment, we must establish under which semantics the different queries we study are evaluated.
This choice determines whether and how duplicate results can arise, which directly have an impact how the query containment resolution.
We distinguish three cases: Single endpoint, federated queries with \texttt{SERVICE} clauses (\ac{UDF}), and federated queries under automatic source selection (\ac{ESA} and \ac{ASSF}).

\subsection{Single Endpoint Execution}
SPARQL queries on single sources are evaluated under bag-set semantics.
In SPARQL queries, the underlying \ac{KG} operates under set semantics~\cite{w3ConceptsAbstract}, while SPARQL operators produce bags\footnote{Operators that can produce duplicates.}.
The choice between bag and set semantics has a significant impact on the complexity and decidability of query containment~\cite{Afrati2010}.\footnote{This repository presents a demonstration showing when bag semantics affect the correctness of query containment in SPARQL. \url{https://github.com/constraintAutomaton/demo-semantic-query-containment-SPARQL}}
In the context of \ac{CQ} (ignoring \texttt{VALUES} and \texttt{BIND} clauses), only projection~\cite{Afrati2010} can increase the multiplicity.
In the context of \ac{UCQ}, the multiplicity can additionally be increased by \texttt{UNION} and by the combination of \texttt{UNION} and \texttt{JOIN}, as defined in \Cref{def_union_multiplicity,def_join_multiplicity}.
Hence \texttt{JOIN} cannot on its own be a source of duplicate solutions.
Because the underlying \ac{KG} is under set semantics, every input mapping has multiplicity one, so their product remains one unless a \texttt{UNION} in the context of \ac{UCQ} has already introduced higher multiplicities.
\ac{UCQ} is not the subject of our study, however, the following sections show how a \ac{CQ} issued by the user can become a \ac{UCQ} during federated execution.
In the context of federated queries, where multiple \acp{KG} are involved, the semantics of query processing are less straightforward.
The following sections detail the semantics of each federation paradigm.

\subsection{Federated Queries With \texttt{SERVICE} Clauses (\ac{UDF})}
The SPARQL federated query extension~\cite{w3SPARQLFederated} defines the execution of federated queries with \texttt{SERVICE} clauses as a join between the bag of solution mappings from the preceding execution and the bag of solution mappings returned by the SPARQL service.
\Cref{List:pseudoCodeFederatedQuery} presents the pseudo-code of this operation as taken from the specification~\cite{w3SPARQLFederated}.
Since in \ac{CQ} join operations in the SPARQL specification cannot increase the multiplicity of a result bag~\cite[Definition: Join]{w3SPARQLQuery}, duplicate results cannot arise from services returning the same answer, and query processing therefore remains under bag-set semantics.

  [...]
  If E is of the form SERVICE [SILENT] IRI {P}
    Let G := Join(G, Service(IRI, Transform(P), SilentOp))
  End
End

The result is G.

\subsection{Federated Queries Using Automatic Source Selection}\label{sec_fed_query_without_service}
Federated queries without \texttt{SERVICE} clauses (also called federated queries with automatic source selection) are not defined in the SPARQL specification, but have been formalized in the literature by \citeauthor{Cheng2021}~\cite{Cheng2021}.

The \ac{ESA} can be considered the default strategy for source selection in federated queries without \texttt{SERVICE} clauses.
Source selection algorithms determine which federation members should receive query subsets without losing results or causing unproductive computation.
When information is insufficient to make this determination, or when multiple federation members could contribute to a query subset's results, the \ac{ESA} principle applies~\cite{Cheng2021}.\footnote{A query engine may choose to use the \ac{ESA} strategy for its simplicity or to avoid issuing too many requests to federation members.}
\ac{ESA} is defined by the following equation expressed using the FedQPL formalism~\cite{Cheng2021}:

\begin{equation}
  \fmj\bigl(\fmu(\freq_{\mathit{fm}_1}^{\mathit{tp}_1}, \dots, \freq_{\mathit{fm}_m}^{\mathit{tp}_1}), \dots, \fmu(\freq_{\mathit{fm}_1}^{\mathit{tp}_n}, \dots, \freq_{\mathit{fm}_m}^{\mathit{tp}_n})\bigr)
  \label{eq_esa}
\end{equation}

where $\fmj$ denotes a join operation over its arguments, $\fmu$ denotes a union operation over its arguments, and $\freq$ denotes the query operation of $\mathit{tp}_i$ against federation member $\mathit{fm}_j$.

The above equation reveals that a \ac{CQ} involving at least two federation members becomes a \ac{UCQ} under \ac{ESA}, since each triple pattern is distributed to all members and their results are combined via \texttt{UNION}.
This structural shift creates a challenge for query containment as \ac{UCQ} containment under bag-set semantics is undecidable and no decidable fragments have been documented.
We therefore propose the \ac{BFR}, a transformation showing that \ac{ESA} evaluation is semantically equivalent to evaluating the original \ac{CQ} against a \ac{BKG} under bag semantics.
