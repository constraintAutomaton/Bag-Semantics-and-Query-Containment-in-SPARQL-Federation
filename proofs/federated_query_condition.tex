\paragraph{Intuition.}
Classical containment asks whether $Q_1$'s results are a subbag of $Q_2$'s on every database.
Federated containment must additionally track \emph{where} each triple pattern is evaluated, because different federation members hold independent data.
If $Q_1$ evaluates a pattern at federation member $\mathit{:a}$ and $Q_2$ evaluates the same pattern at a different member $\mathit{:b}$, no containment claim can be made without knowing the relationship between their contents---a relationship this paper does not assume.
Locations record this distinction inside the query: two triple patterns that differ only in their location are distinct subgoals.
The databases over which a federated query is evaluated are then exactly the located databases of \Cref{def_located_db}, so containment of federated queries becomes containment of their located forms.

Because federation members hold independent data, a shortfall at one location cannot be compensated at another: if the triple patterns $Q_1$ evaluates at some member return results that are not a subbag of those $Q_2$ evaluates at the same member, no other part of $Q_2$ can make up the difference.
Conversely, if $Q_2$ were to contact a federation member $f \notin F_1$, that extra \texttt{SERVICE} call would be joined into $Q_2$'s result; since $f$'s data is independent, it may have no compatible binding for some solution mappings, dropping them from $Q_2$ (in the extreme, emptying it) on databases where $Q_1$ still returns them, breaking containment.
Containment therefore requires $F_2 \subseteq F_1$.
The converse is not required: $Q_1$ may address a member that $Q_2$ does not, as joining the results of that member can only discard solution mappings from $Q_1$---unless the member binds a variable that the head projects away, in which case it duplicates them instead.

Containment of individual \texttt{SERVICE} calls alone is not sufficient: even if each call of the contained query is contained in the corresponding call of the containing query, the queries are not contained if the combining operation differs---a join in one and a union in the other yield incomparable result bags.
For queries combining subqueries by \texttt{UNION} over federation members (\acp{UCFQ}), the \ac{BFR} (\Cref{sec_bag_federation_reduction}) must first be applied: each \ac{UCFQ} is rewritten as a \ac{CQ} over a \ac{BKG}, and containment then reduces to \ac{CQ} bag-semantic containment over that \ac{BKG}, a decidable problem addressed by the results in \Cref{tab_decidable_containment_bag_bag_set}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop_federated_containment}
Let $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ be flattened federated \acp{CQ} over the federations $F_1$ and $F_2$, and let $L(Q)$ denote the located form of a query $Q$.
Then
\[
Q_1 \sqsubseteq Q_2 \iff L(Q_1) \sqsubseteq L(Q_2),
\]
where $\sqsubseteq$ on result bags denotes bag containment, and the right-hand side is containment of \acp{CQ} over located databases.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every federation determines a located database (\Cref{def_located_db}), and conversely every located database over $F_1 \cup F_2$ arises from a federation, obtained by grouping its located triples by their location.

Moreover $\eval{Q}{D} = \eval{L(Q)}{D^{*}}$ for every flattened federated \ac{CQ} $Q$ and every federation $D$ with located database $D^{*}$.
By \Cref{def_located_form}, a subgoal of $L(Q)$ whose location is $f$ matches in $D^{*}$ exactly the located triples whose location is $f$, hence exactly the triples that the corresponding triple pattern of $Q$ matches in the \ac{KG} $G_f$ of that member.
Locations affect neither compatibility (\Cref{def_merge}) nor the multiplicity of a join (\Cref{def_join_multiplicity}), and $Q$ and $L(Q)$ have the same head, so both evaluations produce the same solution mappings with the same multiplicities.

Suppose $Q_1 \sqsubseteq Q_2$ but $L(Q_1) \not\sqsubseteq L(Q_2)$.
Then there is an assignment of a \ac{KG} to each member of $F_1 \cup F_2$ such that, on its located database, the result bag of $L(Q_1)$ is not a subbag of that of $L(Q_2)$, whereas on that assignment the result bag of $Q_1$ is a subbag of that of $Q_2$.
The located forms would therefore return different result bags from the queries themselves, contradicting the above.
The same argument shows that the containment of the located forms implies the containment of the queries.
\end{proof}

It is worth noting interesting properties arising from the containment conditions.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor_fed_subset}
  Let $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ be flattened federated \acp{CQ} over the federations $F_1$ and $F_2$.
  If $Q_1 \sqsubseteq Q_2$, then $F_2 \subseteq F_1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Let $f \in F_2 \setminus F_1$, and assign the empty \ac{KG} to $f$ and to every other federation member a \ac{KG} on which $Q_1$ returns a non-empty bag, which exists as a \ac{CQ} is satisfiable.
  The part of $Q_2$ evaluated at $f$ then returns no solution mapping, and the join with the remaining parts returns none either, so $Q_2$ returns the empty bag.
  As $f \notin F_1$, the assignment leaves $Q_1$ unaffected and it returns a non-empty bag, hence $Q_1 \not\sqsubseteq Q_2$.
\end{proof}

For a federation member $f$, we write $Q|_f$ for the part of $Q$ evaluated at $f$, and
\[
  \operatorname{fresh}(Q, f) = \operatorname{vars}(Q|_f) \setminus \Bigl( \operatorname{vars}(\operatorname{head}(Q)) \cup \bigcup_{g \neq f} \operatorname{vars}(Q|_g) \Bigr)
\]
for the \emph{fresh} variables of $f$ in $Q$, those it binds alone and the head discards.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor_fed_extra_member}
  Let $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ be flattened federated \acp{CQ} over the federations $F_1$ and $F_2$, whose members are endpoints.
  If $Q_1 \sqsubseteq Q_2$, then $\operatorname{fresh}(Q_1, f) = \emptyset$ for every $f \in F_1 \setminus F_2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Let $v \in \operatorname{fresh}(Q_1, f)$ for some $f \in F_1 \setminus F_2$, and take an assignment of \acp{KG} on which $Q_1$ returns a non-empty bag.
  As $f \notin F_2$, no part of $Q_2$ is evaluated at $f$ and the result of $Q_2$ does not depend on the \ac{KG} of $f$.
  Extending that \ac{KG} with copies of the triples matching $Q_1|_f$ that differ only in the value bound to $v$ multiplies the multiplicity of every solution mapping of $Q_1$ by the number of copies, since $v$ occurs in no other part and the head discards it, while leaving $Q_2$ unchanged.
  Taking more copies than the multiplicity of $Q_2$ breaks the containment, as illustrated in \Cref{fig:extra-member-cases}~(a).
\end{proof}

\Cref{fig:extra-member-cases}~(b) shows the same pair with \texttt{?job} in the head, where the extra member binds no fresh variable and duplicates no solution mapping, so the condition of \Cref{cor_fed_extra_member} holds.
That pair is still not contained, as the two queries read \texttt{?job} at different federation members, which shows the condition to be necessary and not sufficient.

Both properties depend only on the \acp{IRI} of the federation members and on the variables of the two queries, so they can be checked before deciding containment.
They also characterize refinement, a common way of constructing federated queries: adding a \texttt{SERVICE} clause to a query yields a contained query exactly when that clause binds no fresh variable, and duplicates its solution mappings otherwise.

\begin{remark}\label{rem_variable_location}
  The located form accommodates locations that are variables, as a located triple pattern whose fourth component is a variable is still the subgoal of an ordinary \ac{CQ}.
  \Cref{cor_fed_subset,cor_fed_extra_member} do not carry over, as both rely on a location being a constant that identifies a single federation member, and containment then quantifies over which federation members exist rather than over their content alone.
  We leave such queries out of scope, as the SPARQL specification gives no official interpretation to \texttt{SERVICE} clauses with variable endpoint URLs (\Cref{sec:related_works_fed}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}\label{rem_ucfq_located}
  A sub-federation can itself be treated as a federation member, virtual rather than an endpoint, whose graph is the \ac{BKG} of that sub-federation (\Cref{def_bkg}).
  The \ac{BFR} therefore yields a flattened federated \ac{CQ} over a federation containing virtual members.
  \Cref{prop_federated_containment} applies to such a query, so containment of \acp{UCFQ} is decided by first applying the \ac{BFR} and then taking the located form.
  The order matters, as the located form of a \ac{UCFQ} still contains \texttt{UNION} and is therefore not a \ac{CQ}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}\label{rem_located_db_bag}
  Whether a located database is a set or a bag depends on the members of the federation.
  If every member is an endpoint, each $G_f$ is a set and so is the located database; containment is then under bag-set semantics, and the head projection is the only operator that can raise a multiplicity (\Cref{sec:semantic_federated_q}).
  If some member is virtual, its graph is a \ac{BKG} and therefore a bag, as is the located database; containment is then under bag semantics, and multiplicities can arise from the data itself.
\end{remark}
